# Neuroscientists Have Confirmed Our Suspicions | Pushka. Brain

> This article follows the evidence behind a familiar story, then turns it into a practical way to read the system, its limits, and the decision a careful reader can make next.

This science digest, published in Russian, covers infrasound, anesthesia, cancer, and spinal repair. Across these topics, it repeatedly finds the same pattern: hidden control systems act before consciousness can report them.

![Textless editorial image of hidden sound waves moving through the body before conscious awareness.](assets/generated-bitmap-v1/hidden-body-signals-hero-overlay.png)

The first clue is a fencing sword shaking on its own. Vic Tandy worked around medical equipment and had little patience for ghost stories. His colleagues said the lab felt wrong. They felt watched. Some saw a grey figure at the edge of their vision.

Tandy laughed at these reports until he stayed late at the lab. Dread flooded his body. He saw the same figure at the edge of his vision.

He began sweating in a cold room. Afterward, a blade held in a vise began to tremble.

![A haunted laboratory becomes an engineering problem.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/01-ghost-machine-paper.jpg)

Tandy moved a table and observed the vibration of a blade rise and fall. He traced the dread felt in the room to a standing wave of infrasound produced by ventilation. Infrasound is a pressure too low for ordinary hearing.

The infrasound interacted with the geometry of the room to produce a standing wave. The case became the classic [Tandy and Lawrence "ghost in the machine" account](https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf). The Tandy case turned an apparent ghost into a measurable waveform.

A newer [double-blind infrasound experiment](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2026.1729876/full) extended the finding that the body can respond to a signal the mind has not identified. Researchers paired infrasound near 18 Hz with either calm music or anxious ambient sound.

Participants guessed at chance level whether the hidden low sound was present, and their saliva showed a rise in cortisol. Calm music felt gloomier when infrasound was present. Listeners’ emotional state shifted while they remained consciously unaware of the infrasound signal.

![Natural infrasound turns the invisible signal into weather-scale machinery.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/03-spectrogram-response.jpg)

The episode followed a fixed sequence. A signal first arrived below reportable level. The organism’s state then changed.

After that, consciousness generated a caption describing the change. The caption sometimes named a ghost, sometimes a bad mood, sometimes no explanation at all.

## The hippocampus keeps reading after the lights go out

The older model of the brain gives consciousness a central executive role: it notices, decides, remembers, and explains. A newer view compares the brain to a building at night, where the front desk is closed while some departments keep working.

Seven epilepsy patients received propofol anesthesia during surgery. Neuropixels probes were placed deep enough in these patients to record individual cells in the hippocampus. A [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10448-0) reports the study.

The recording window was about ten to twenty minutes per patient, according to the [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10448-0). Access to record from the hippocampus this way is rare. The study asked what happens when speech and sound enter a brain whose owner will later remember none of it.

![The hippocampus study becomes the hinge of the article.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/04-hippocampus-diagram.jpg)

In the experiment, patients first heard repeated tones with occasional deviant, or oddball, tones. Hippocampal neurons adapted to the repeated tones and learned the pattern well enough to flag oddballs as exceptions.

Patients were next played speech. Hippocampal cells tracked word meanings, grammatical roles, and the probability of which word might come next. Words with similar meanings produced related neural activity patterns. The patients were anesthetized and remembered nothing from the speech stream. Despite this lack of memory, the hippocampus still processed language in a structured way. A [Scientific American summary](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-brain-processes-overheard-words-under-anesthesia-but-it-may-not-remember-them/) described the result as overheard words being processed under anesthesia without later recall.

![Surgical sleep leaves some high-level processing active.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/05-surgical-sleep-mri.jpg)

This view is narrower than hidden wakefulness under anesthesia. It does not require secret waking.

Some brain regions may continue to process language while the system that makes experience belong to a person is inactive. A sentence can be processed without being experienced as one's own.

The anesthesia case suggests something about the self. Consciousness may be less the part doing the work than a layer binding distributed processing into one remembered scene.

When binding shuts down, processing can continue.

## A dream can spend the night on your behalf

Sleep is expected to be the period in which the body repairs itself. Some dreams function more like a second job than like rest. This episode discusses hyperoneirism, also called epic dreaming. It involves long, vivid, continuous dream experiences. People with hyperoneirism feel exhausted afterward, even when standard sleep measures appear normal.

Patients report nights of dreams containing plots, missions, conversations, travel, conflict, and obligation. They wake feeling as though they have already worked a shift.

![Epic dreaming becomes easier to grasp when it stops being abstract.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/07-epic-dream-illustration.jpg)

A patient can have normal sleep-chart results. Breathing, muscle activity, and sleep stages can appear normal. The patient can still wake with a physical cost from the night.

Medicine often sees cases where a patient experiences a pattern before clinicians have a clean instrument for it. The private report comes first, classification follows, and treatment comes after that.

Epic dreaming matters because it makes a hidden layer of sleep costly. During it, the body spends the night inside a story, and the person's waking self inherits the result by morning.

## Cancer is a control-system failure

The medical-news section continues the same argument from perception to tissue. Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is a disease where optimism has to be earned in small steps. It is often detected late. It spreads fast. It has resisted many therapies that changed other cancers. The daraxonrasib result had an unusually strong impact because of this context.

Daraxonrasib is an oral, multi-selective RAS(ON) inhibitor. In the Phase 3 RASolute 302 trial in previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer, median overall survival was about 13.2 months with daraxonrasib. [Phase 3 RASolute 302 result](https://ir.revmed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/daraxonrasib-demonstrates-unprecedented-overall-survival-benefit) In the same trial, median overall survival was about 6.7 months with standard chemotherapy. [Phase 3 RASolute 302 result](https://ir.revmed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/daraxonrasib-demonstrates-unprecedented-overall-survival-benefit)

ASCO's conference release makes the central point that this is a serious survival signal in a very hard disease. [conference release](https://www.asco.org/about-asco/press-center/multi-selective-ras-inhibitor-nearly-doubles-survival-pancreatic-cancer) Dana-Farber's clinical summary makes the same central point that this is a serious survival signal in a very hard disease. [clinical summary](https://www.dana-farber.org/newsroom/news-releases/2026/rason-inhibitor-doubles-median-overall-survival-in-results-of-phase-3-trial-for-patients-with-metastatic-pancreatic-cancer)

![Textless editorial image of a tumor control network.](assets/generated-bitmap-v1/tumor-control-network.png)

The treatment claim has two parts. Both are stated. In this disease context, a near doubling of survival is a very large effect. The survival endpoint is still measured in months.

The result is described soberly: a handle on a target once considered almost undruggable. The deeper lesson is system architecture. Cancer is a broken control system involving growth signals, DNA repair, immune evasion, tissue pressure, nutrient routes, local signaling, and evolutionary selection.

A drug matters when it reaches a control node. A small molecule can change a map.

## A tumor can change the neighborhood rules

Chromosomal instability means that cells have abnormal chromosome numbers or structures. It resembles damage, yet in cancer it can work to the tumor’s advantage. These abnormalities give tumors variation to search with, including extra growth genes, missing suppressors, and unstable combinations.

The episode’s next paper describes an additional territorial behavior of tumors. Some chromosomally unstable tumor cells enter a senescent-like state. They broadcast signals that push neighboring healthy cells to slow down or die. IRB Barcelona’s [study summary](https://www.irbbarcelona.org/en/news/scientific/study-flies-reveals-how-some-tumours-eliminate-healthy-neighbouring-cells-grow) describes the work as a fruit-fly model in which tumors clear room around themselves.

![The tumor paper shifts the story from growth to territory.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/09-embo-tumor-paper.jpg)

A senescent cell is expected to stop dividing. It remains active, though. Its activity makes the surrounding environment worse for normal tissue and better for a tumor.

![The animated tumor is crude, but the mechanism is memorable: clear space, gain room.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/10-cancer-cell-graphic.jpg)

Supporting evidence comes from fruit-fly experiments. Whether the findings apply to humans remains unresolved.

The conceptual shift matters. The question moves from faults inside the malignant cell to how it changes the rules of its surrounding environment. A tumor can behave like poor governance.

## A tiny gland can lock a whole life state

A woman has one child, stops breastfeeding, and then develops secondary infertility. After she stopped breastfeeding, her body kept producing milk.

She also has headaches, night sweats, and fatigue. Prolactin is a hormone that helps drive lactation.

Excess prolactin can suppress ovulation. The source of the excess prolactin is a pituitary tumor at the base of the brain.

When the tumor is removed, prolactin returns to normal levels and pregnancy becomes possible again. The case takes up little screen time, but it illustrates a control system well.

A tiny gland can set a reproductive state affecting the whole body. A signal for lactation can act as a lock on fertility. A local mass can become a problem that affects the course of a person's life. Bodies have dashboards, but people mostly experience only the output.

## Repair may be a locked program

The spinal-cord section is where careless writing is most risky, because the hope it raises is so obvious. Researchers built human organoid circuits that model connections between brain tissue and spinal cord tissue. During development, neurons grow axons with considerable freedom.

Mature central nervous system neurons lose much of that ability. A [Cell Reports study](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247%2826%2900477-8) identified a program that restrains axon regrowth. The same [Cell Reports study](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247%2826%2900477-8) then screened for ways to loosen the regrowth-restraining program.

![Textless editorial image of axons regrowing across an organoid culture.](assets/generated-bitmap-v1/axon-regrowth-organoid.png)

Lynestrenol is a synthetic progestin used as a contraceptive. In the organoid model, lynestrenol improved axon growth. The lab's public explanation of the lynestrenol finding was also covered by [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260528082459.htm).

The lynestrenol result comes from a dish model, which is far from an injured human spinal cord. Real spinal cord injury adds scar tissue, inflammation, and broken tissue architecture. The hardest problem in real injury is reconnecting the right neurons to the right places.

Adult neurons may carry a brake on repair. If the repair brake can be mapped, therapy becomes less mysterious. Therapy would then involve timing, guidance, scaffolding, chemistry, and targeted release of a developmental program.

Hope is stronger when it is specific rather than vague.

## The hidden layer keeps winning

This episode is a digest that moves across a haunted lab, infrasound, anesthesia, dreams, pancreatic cancer, tumor ecology, prolactin, and spinal repair.

The common thread is a hidden control layer. Vibration can alter stress before anyone perceives it as sound. The hippocampus processes language before memory receives a scene.

A dream uses up the night before the waking self can object to it. A cancer cell changes the rules governing tissue. A pituitary signal suppresses reproduction.

A mature neuron has a brake that a developing neuron lacks. The self a person can narrate is real, but it arrives late.

Most of the body runs on older regulatory machinery: sensors, thresholds, switches, chemical signals, emergency policies, developmental locks, local negotiations, and inherited evolutionary leftovers that still operate.

Consciousness remains part of the picture, as the layer that makes a world reportable after deeper systems have already acted. A person is a layered organism, and many layers are already processing information before the conscious person arrives.
