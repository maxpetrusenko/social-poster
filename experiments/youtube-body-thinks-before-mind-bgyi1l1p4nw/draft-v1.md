# Neuroscientists Have Confirmed Our Suspicions | Pushka. Brain

> This article follows the evidence behind a familiar story, then turns it into a practical way to read the system, its limits, and the decision a careful reader can make next.

*A Russian science digest moves from infrasound and anesthesia to cancer and spinal repair, and keeps finding the same pattern: hidden control systems act before consciousness can report them.*

![Textless editorial image of hidden sound waves moving through the body before conscious awareness.](assets/generated-bitmap-v1/hidden-body-signals-hero-overlay.png)

The first clue is a fencing sword shaking by itself.

Vic Tandy worked around medical equipment and had little patience for ghost stories. His colleagues said the lab felt wrong. They felt watched. Some saw a grey figure at the edge of vision. Tandy laughed until he stayed late, felt dread flood his body, saw the same edge-of-eye figure, and started sweating in a cold room.

Then a blade in a vise began to tremble.

![A haunted laboratory becomes an engineering problem.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/01-ghost-machine-paper.jpg)

An engineer can respect fear without accepting the explanation fear hands him. Tandy moved the table, watched the blade's vibration rise and fall, and traced the room's dread to a standing wave of infrasound from ventilation. A pressure too low for ordinary hearing had found the room's geometry. The case became the classic [Tandy and Lawrence "ghost in the machine" account](https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf).

A ghost had become a waveform. The next part reached further.

The body can receive a fact before the mind can name it.

A newer [double-blind infrasound experiment](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2026.1729876/full) pushed this further. Researchers paired near-18 Hz infrasound with calm music or anxious ambient sound. People guessed whether the hidden low sound was present at chance level. Their saliva gave a cleaner answer. Cortisol rose. Calm music felt gloomier. The room's emotional weather shifted while the listeners stayed consciously deaf to the signal.

![Natural infrasound turns the invisible signal into weather-scale machinery.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/03-spectrogram-response.jpg)

The episode followed the same sequence. A signal enters below the reportable layer. The organism changes state. Then consciousness writes a caption.

Sometimes the caption says ghost. Sometimes it says bad mood. Sometimes it says nothing at all.

## The hippocampus keeps reading after the lights go out

The old model gives consciousness the executive office. We notice, decide, remember, and explain.

The newer picture looks more like a building at night. The front desk is closed. Some departments keep working.

During surgery, seven epilepsy patients under propofol anesthesia had Neuropixels probes placed deep enough to record individual cells in the hippocampus. The [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10448-0) had a short window, about ten to twenty minutes per patient. The access was rare. The question was clean: what happens when speech and sound enter a brain whose owner will later remember none of it?

![The hippocampus study becomes the hinge of the article.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/04-hippocampus-diagram.jpg)

First came repeated tones with occasional oddballs. Hippocampal neurons adapted. They learned the pattern enough to mark the exception.

Then came speech. The cells tracked word meaning, grammatical roles, and the probability of what might come next. Words close together in meaning produced related neural patterns. The anesthetized patients remembered nothing from the stream. The hippocampus still handled language as structure; a [Scientific American summary](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-brain-processes-overheard-words-under-anesthesia-but-it-may-not-remember-them/) framed the result around overheard words being processed without later recall.

![Surgical sleep leaves some high-level processing active.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/05-surgical-sleep-mri.jpg)

The colder version is narrower than hidden wakefulness. Parts of the brain can keep parsing while the system that makes experience ownable is offline.

A sentence can be processed without becoming your sentence.

The anesthesia story points at the self. Maybe consciousness is less the worker than the binding layer that turns distributed work into a single remembered scene.

When the binder closes, the pages can still move.

## A dream can spend the night on your behalf

Sleep should be the body's repair shift. Some dreams behave like a second job.

The episode turns to hyperoneirism, also described as epic dreaming: long, vivid, continuous dream experiences that leave people exhausted even when standard sleep measures look ordinary. Patients report nights with plots, missions, conversations, travel, conflict, obligation. They wake as if they have already worked a shift.

![Epic dreaming becomes easier to grasp when it stops being abstract.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/07-epic-dream-illustration.jpg)

The unsettling detail is the normal chart. Breathing can look fine. Muscle activity can look fine. Sleep stages can look fine. The patient still wakes carrying a physical debt.

Medicine repeatedly encounters this blind spot. A patient lives inside a pattern before the clinic has a clean instrument for it. The private report comes first. Classification comes later. Treatment comes later still.

Epic dreaming matters because it makes the hidden layer expensive. The body spends the night in a story the morning self inherits.

## Cancer is a control-system failure

The medical-news section looks at first like a hard turn. It carries the same argument from perception to tissue.

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is one of the diseases where optimism has to earn every inch. It is often caught late, spreads fast, and has resisted many of the therapies that changed other cancers. The daraxonrasib result therefore landed with unusual force.

Daraxonrasib is an oral multi-selective RAS(ON) inhibitor. In the [Phase 3 RASolute 302 result](https://ir.revmed.com/news-releases/news-release-details/daraxonrasib-demonstrates-unprecedented-overall-survival-benefit) for previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer, median overall survival was about 13.2 months with daraxonrasib versus about 6.7 months with standard chemotherapy. ASCO's [conference release](https://www.asco.org/about-asco/press-center/multi-selective-ras-inhibitor-nearly-doubles-survival-pancreatic-cancer) and Dana-Farber's [clinical summary](https://www.dana-farber.org/newsroom/news-releases/2026/rason-inhibitor-doubles-median-overall-survival-in-results-of-phase-3-trial-for-patients-with-metastatic-pancreatic-cancer) make the same central point: this is a serious survival signal in a brutal disease.

![Textless editorial image of a tumor control network.](assets/generated-bitmap-v1/tumor-control-network.png)

The sentence needs both halves. Nearly doubled survival is enormous in this context. The endpoint is still measured in months. This is sober language: a handle appearing on a target once treated as almost undruggable.

The deeper lesson is architectural. Cancer is a broken control system: growth signals, DNA repair, immune hiding, tissue pressure, nutrient routes, local signaling, evolutionary selection. A drug matters when it reaches a control node.

A small molecule can change a map.

## A tumor can change the neighborhood rules

Chromosomal instability sounds like damage. In cancer, damage can become strategy.

When cells carry abnormal chromosome numbers or structures, tumors get variation to search with: extra growth genes, missing suppressors, unstable combinations. The episode's next paper adds a more territorial behavior. Some chromosomally unstable tumor cells enter a senescent-like state, then broadcast signals that push neighboring healthy cells to slow down or die. IRB Barcelona's [study summary](https://www.irbbarcelona.org/en/news/scientific/study-flies-reveals-how-some-tumours-eliminate-healthy-neighbouring-cells-grow) describes the work as a fruit-fly model of tumors clearing room around themselves.

![The tumor paper shifts the story from growth to territory.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/09-embo-tumor-paper.jpg)

A senescent cell is supposed to stop dividing. These cells stop and still act. They make the neighborhood worse for normal tissue and better for the tumor.

![The animated tumor is crude, but the mechanism is memorable: clear space, gain room.](assets/youtube-frames/selected-v3/10-cancer-cell-graphic.jpg)

The evidence is from fruit flies, so the human translation stays open. The conceptual move is still important. The question shifts from what is wrong inside the malignant cell to how the malignant cell rewrites the rules around it.

A tumor can behave like bad governance.

## A tiny gland can lock a whole life state

Then the scale shrinks again.

A woman has one child, stops breastfeeding, and enters secondary infertility. Milk production continues. Headaches arrive. Night sweats. Fatigue. The clue is prolactin, the hormone that helps drive lactation. Too much prolactin can suppress ovulation. The source is a pituitary tumor at the base of the brain.

Remove the tumor, prolactin returns to normal, and pregnancy becomes possible again.

The case is small only in screen time. It is a perfect control-system story. A tiny gland can set a body-wide reproductive state. A lactation signal becomes a fertility lock. A local mass becomes a life-path problem.

Bodies have dashboards. We mostly live in the output.

## Repair may be a locked program

The spinal-cord section is the most dangerous one for sloppy writing because the hope is so obvious.

Researchers built human organoid circuits that model links between brain and spinal cord tissue. During development, neurons grow axons with impressive freedom. Mature central nervous system neurons lose much of that ability. The [Cell Reports study](https://www.cell.com/cell-reports/fulltext/S2211-1247%2826%2900477-8) identified a program that restrains regrowth, then screened for ways to loosen it.

![Textless editorial image of axons regrowing across an organoid culture.](assets/generated-bitmap-v1/axon-regrowth-organoid.png)

One surprise was lynestrenol, a synthetic progestin used as a contraceptive. In the organoid model, it improved axon growth; the lab's public explanation was also covered by [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260528082459.htm).

The boundary matters. This remains a dish model, far from an injured human spinal cord. Real injury adds scar tissue, inflammation, broken architecture, and the hardest problem: reconnecting the right neurons to the right places.

The serious hope is more specific. Adult neurons may carry a repair brake. If the brake can be mapped, therapy becomes less mystical. It becomes timing, guidance, scaffolding, chemistry, and targeted release of a developmental program.

Hope gets stronger when it stops being vague.

## The hidden layer keeps winning

The episode is a digest, so it jumps: haunted lab, infrasound, anesthesia, dreams, pancreatic cancer, tumor ecology, prolactin, spinal repair.

The through-line is the control layer.

A vibration changes stress before it becomes sound. A hippocampus parses language before memory receives a scene. A dream spends the night before the morning self can object. A cancer cell changes tissue law. A pituitary signal locks reproduction. A mature neuron carries a brake that a developing neuron does not.

The self you can narrate is real. It is also late.

Most of the body is older bureaucracy: sensors, thresholds, switches, chemical memos, emergency policies, developmental locks, local negotiations, dead letters from evolution that still get delivered. Consciousness stays in the picture and becomes more interesting: the layer that makes a world reportable after deeper systems have already moved.

You are a layered organism, and many of the layers are already thinking before you arrive.
