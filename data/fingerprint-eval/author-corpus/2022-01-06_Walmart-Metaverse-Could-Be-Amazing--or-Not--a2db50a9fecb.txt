## Walmart Metaverse Could Be Amazing (or Not)

### Is shopping from your house good or bad?

> I’ve often felt like shopping would be more enjoyable if i was accompanied by the floating ghost of a retail worker whose pronouncements are periodically drowned out by the hellish sounds of clanking machinery

One big thing could be VR shopping. Imagine you are taking a virtual tour around the isle, picking up an object and looking at it from any angle, seeing the size and scale of it. Small, 2D images don’t work as well. A lot of the time we put some faith and trust that this is the item we want but end up returning it back to amazon.

On another side, you can feel a serious lack of imagination. Not sure VR is the best place for groceries shopping. But it is an interesting concept which opens up a lot of creative ideas, like IKEA app where you could try different furniture in your home.

But some people will prefer a simple web page. VR doesn’t have to be an all-around replacement for everything.

If the Internet history taught us anything, it’s that translating real-world retail spaces into virtual ones didn’t work. What worked for us was a large searchable database of products that took one to two clicks to buy.

The virtual experience is relevant only when it adds utility — test driving cars; teaching surgeons new procedures that can determine their level of competence before they operate on patients; trying on virtual clothes. There are tons of potential use cases for VR and shopping, buying Cola might not be one of them.

I remember a time when I was told that it is “wrong to live in a fantasy world” and that my hobbies and interests were “unhealthy escapism”, computer games are not real, and so on…But as it turns out all they were really mad because the technology to properly monetize imagination wasn’t there.
