## What can you learn here

- The transaction was initiated by the owner's wallet directly.
- It is not a contract approval scam

The ‘From’ field is the owner's wallet address. This means the transaction was initiated by OP’s wallet directly.

The ‘To’ field says it’s a contract, but the contract name is ‘Centre: USD Coin’ and the address is the address of the USDC token. So this is a direct transfer of the token, and there is no third-party contract involved here.

If it was an approval scam, what we would expect to see is:

The ‘From’ field could be OP’s wallet address, or it could be a third party address. Either option is possible with an approval scam.

The ‘To’ field would NOT be the USDC contract, this would be some random contract, probably unnamed.

Disclaimer: always double-check the address you are sending to.

When you approve a contract to spend tokens, transactions that spend the token need to be going through that contract(approval). Some wallet needs to call a function on that contract, which will transfer the tokens.

If you look at all the transactions with the money going into the thief’s account, there is no third-party contract involved:

The transactions are simple sends that are not interacting with any third-party contract, transferring directly from the owner's wallet to the thief’s wallet. There is no approval scam here.

### Do not’s

- Don’t write your private keys in any file on any electronic device.
- Do not give your seed phrase to anyone (including metamask support).
- Don’t “verify your wallet” on any website
- Don’t make photos of your private keys
- Don’t copy-paste your private keys
- Do not use public networks.
- Do not leave your metamask unlocked.

### Do’s

- Move your crypto to a cold wallet/ ledger
- Write private keys on a piece of paper.
- Use auto-lock timer
- Always use Google Authenticator (or similar) for 2-factor security on exchanges

## Final thoughts

Giving your secret phrase away will let people access your crypto regardless of anything else.

Metamask will never ask you for your seed phrase.

Being your own bank is only safer if you put the work in. Otherwise, it can be a nightmare.
