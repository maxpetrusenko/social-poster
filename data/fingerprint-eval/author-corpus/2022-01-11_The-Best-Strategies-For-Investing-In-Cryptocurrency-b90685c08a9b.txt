## The Best Strategies For Investing In Cryptocurrency

### Hodling, Dollar-Cost Averaging, and Lump Sum Investment

There are three most popular investing strategies: Hodl, Dollar-Cost Averaging, and Lump Sum Investment. Let’s talk more about them.

## What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)?

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is an investment strategy in which an investor divides up the total amount to be invested across periodic purchases of a target asset in an effort to reduce the impact of volatility on the overall purchase. The purchases occur regardless of the asset’s price and at regular intervals. In effect, this strategy removes much of the detailed work of attempting to time the market in order to make purchases of equities at the best prices.

### Why do some investors use dollar-cost averaging?

The key advantage of dollar-cost averaging is that it reduces the effects of investor psychology and market timing on their portfolio. By committing to a dollar-cost averaging approach, investors avoid the risk that they will make counter-productive decisions out of greed or fear, such as buying more when prices are rising or panic-selling when prices decline. Instead, dollar-cost averaging forces investors to focus on contributing a set amount of money each period while ignoring the price of each individual purchase.

The purpose of DCA is to protect yourself from volatile changes in the market.

When DCA, less profit is gained when the market rises, and fewer losses are incurred when the market sinks. You’ll notice that DCA performs better when markets are down.

## What is Lump-Sum investing?

A lump-sum investment (LSI) is a large sum that is invested in one single transaction instead of broken up into installments. Lump-sum investing generally outperforms because markets tend to rise over the long term.

For investing in any assets = realty, stocks, bitcoin, or gold the best advice is to make a solid plan after doing your research upfront, diversify with uncorrelated asset classes that are properly hedged, and invest all upfront. This is especially true with Bitcoin because no one can predict the price and most appreciation happens on a few days each year that is unexpected so the quicker you own BTC, the quicker you get exposure to this appreciation.

This being said you should not be investing at all in Bitcoin unless you have paid off all your high-interest debt and have at least 3–6 months of fiat in an emergency fund to cover living expenses. Also, make sure that the price is at least 25% from the last time high, which you can wait forever…

## Mathematical perspective

DCA might be a solid strategy for markets going down since your average cost decreases with each investment. That means for markets going up — such as crypto, your average cost is increasing. In fact, this Vanguard study finds that LSI outperforms DCA two-thirds of the time. This is because markets tend to go up more often than they go down. Here’s another chart from Morningstar. The longer the period, the lower the chance of DCA overperforming LSI.

## Behavioral perspective

DCA plan is more complicated than LSI and things happen in life when you either forget to invest or have a change of priorities.

It feels pretty great when your DCA strategy hits dips every time, but what if you keep hitting tops? In reality, it will be a mix of both, but hitting 2–3 tops in a row feels pretty bad. Taking the LSI approach, when you hit the top it’s a one-time event. Hell, zoom out the chart and it might appear like a dip in the long-term perspective.

DCA is not risk-free. You are betting the market will go down for a while, and then come back up later.
